Term Sheet Checklist: 6 Essential Terms, 6 Optional Terms and the Mistakes to Avoid

A practical term sheet checklist: the 6 essential terms every term sheet needs, 6 optional terms to add, and the mistakes to avoid in cross-border deals.

Founders Doc·25 Sep 2026·8 min read

In brief

A good term sheet is short, clear and complete in the places that matter. This term sheet checklist splits the clauses into two groups: six essential terms every term sheet needs, and six optional terms you can add, tweak or leave out depending on the deal.

Use it whether you're raising investment, lending money, buying a business or setting up a partnership, in any country.

These are the terms that make a document a term sheet. If any is missing or unclear, fix it before you send the term sheet.

Part 2: The six optional terms

These are common, but not essential. Include them when they help, and leave them out when they don't.

Optional termInclude it when…Usually binding?
💰 Key commercial termsPrice, valuation or payment terms have been agreedNo
✅ Conditions and due diligenceSomething must happen before closing, such as checks or approvalsNo
📅 TimetableThere are target dates for signing and completionNo
🔒 ExclusivityThe side sending the term sheet needs time to do due diligenceYes
🤐 ConfidentialityAlmost alwaysYes
💸 CostsAlmost always, even if it just says each side pays its ownYes

Key commercial terms by deal type

Include only the terms that have actually been agreed. Common examples:

  • Investment: investment amount, valuation (pre- or post-money), type of shares, board seat, information rights, founder vesting. For SAFEs: valuation cap, discount and MFN.
  • Loan: loan amount, interest rate, term, repayment, security and guarantees.
  • Acquisition: price, price adjustments, deferred payments or earn-out, warranties, key employees, restrictive covenants.
  • Partnership or project: each party's contributions, governance, intellectual property, revenue sharing, term and exit.
FD tip

If the numbers haven't been agreed yet, leave this section out rather than filling it with guesses. A term sheet with only the essential terms is still a valid term sheet.

Part 3: Mistakes to avoid

❌ Mistake✅ Fix
Trading name used as the party nameUse the full registered legal name
"The business" as the subject matterSay exactly what shares, assets or services are involved
No statement of what's bindingAdd a clear legal effect clause
Binding list doesn't match the clauses includedUpdate the list whenever you add or remove a binding clause
No expiry or long-stop dateAdd both, in the right order
"Laws of the United States"Name a state, e.g. "the laws of the State of New York"
Courts of one country, law of anotherMatch law and forum
Vague earn-out ("if the business does well")Use a measurable target and a date
Silence on intellectual property in a development projectSay who owns existing and new IP, even at a high level
Guessing numbers that haven't been agreedLeave them out, or mark them "to be agreed"

For the reasons behind each of these, with examples, see 12 Common Term Sheet Mistakes (and How to Avoid Them).

Part 4: When to get a lawyer involved

A term sheet is short, but some situations need professional advice before you sign:

  • you're asked to make the price or completion binding, or to agree a break fee;
  • the deal involves security, guarantees or a personal guarantee;
  • a party is listed on a stock exchange or regulated;
  • the deal may need regulatory, competition or foreign-investment approval;
  • personal data, customer lists or employees are being transferred;
  • the parties are in different countries and you're unsure about governing law.

Frequently asked questions

What are the most important terms in a term sheet?

The parties, a clear description and structure of the deal, and a statement of what is and isn't binding. Without these, the term sheet can't do its job.

Should a term sheet include the valuation?

If it has been agreed, yes. It's one of the most important commercial terms in an investment. If it hasn't, the term sheet can still cover the essentials and leave valuation for later.

How detailed should a term sheet be?

Detailed enough that nobody is surprised by the final agreements, but no more. If a point needs more than a couple of sentences, it probably belongs in the final agreements.

Can I use one term sheet template for different types of deal?

Yes, if the template separates the essential terms from optional, deal-specific ones. The essentials are the same for every deal; the commercial terms change.

Legal disclaimer. This article is general information, not legal advice. Laws differ between countries, so speak to a lawyer about your specific deal.
